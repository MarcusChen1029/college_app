# Australia: after the offer, student visa, and money (research, 2026-11-04)

Scope: a Taiwan citizen applying from outside Australia for Semester 1 2027 (Feb/Mar 2027) or Semester 2 2027 (July 2027), bachelor's level.
Coverage: everything after an offer, plus money. Application routes, deadlines, entry requirements and medicine tests are another agent's job.

How to read this file
- Every claim has a source URL and the page's own date where the page shows one. "Page dated" = the date printed on the page. "Search summary" = I only saw the claim in a search-result summary and did not read the page text myself; treat as unverified.
- IN FORCE = a page says it applies now. PROPOSED = consultation or announcement only.
- "Not found" / "uncertain" is a real answer. Nothing here was filled from memory.
- Tooling note: Firecrawl credits ran out on 2026-11-04. Pages were read with WebFetch/WebSearch and `curl` (Home Affairs blocks WebFetch with 403 but serves curl; some Home Affairs figures, such as the visa fee, are loaded by script and do not appear in the raw page).
- About 36 tool calls used. No counselor or student-forum pages were read, so Section 6 is built from official warnings, not from community sources (labelled as such).

## 3. Student visa (subclass 500)

### 3.1 Where the rules stand, in force versus proposed

IN FORCE
- Offshore applications lodged on or after 14 November 2025 are processed under Ministerial Direction 115 (MD115); earlier ones under MD111. Home Affairs page dated 22/10/2026: https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500
- Genuine Student (GS) requirement applies to applications lodged on or after 23 March 2024 (replaced Genuine Temporary Entrant). Page dated 24/09/2026: https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500/genuine-student-requirement
- New application rules from 2 October 2026 for the 500 and the Student Guardian 590: they change who can apply while in Australia, student visa holders applying for another student visa, and whether family can be included. Page dated 14/10/2026: https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/changes-to-student-visa-application-rules-500-590 . Mostly affects onshore applicants and families, not a Taiwan school leaver applying offshore alone. Detail of the fact sheet was not read.

ANNOUNCED
- 2027 National Planning Level (NPL): 295,000 new overseas student commencements, the same as 2026; "No active international education provider in Australia will receive a lower allocation in 2027 than it did in 2026"; NPL "is not a cap". Announced 15 July 2026: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/tools-and-resources/news/australia-confirms-international-education-settings-for-2027
- Oversight of higher-education allocations moves to the Australian Tertiary Education Commission from 2027 (search summary of the government joint release, not read).

PROPOSED, NOT IN FORCE
- Home Affairs consultation (page dated 30/10/2026) on (a) updating Ministerial Direction 106, which guides how GS is assessed, (b) strengthening financial-capacity rules, and (c) "a possible interim measure to strengthen financial evidence requirements for primary applicants from Evidence Level 3 countries". "No decisions have been made on the proposals." Submissions close 12:00pm AEDT Monday 23 November 2026. https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-and-support/how-to-engage-us/consultations/consultation-on-proposed-reforms-to-the-genuine-student-requirement-and-financial-capacity-framework
- Implication: the GS wording and the funds figure below could change during the 2027 intakes. A tracker should show a "rules last checked" date.

UNCONFIRMED: whether a different Ministerial Direction number is issued for 2027. The Home Affairs priority page (dated 10/11/2025) already contains 2027 worked examples under MD115, and a search summary says a Ministerial Direction "will be used to continue" prioritisation in 2027. No separate 2027 direction number was found.

### 3.2 Processing priority and how it links to each university's allocation

Source: https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-processing-times/visa-processing-priorities/student-visa (page dated 10/11/2025).
- A Ministerial Direction "is not a visa cap, and it does not set the criteria to approve or refuse". Priority changes speed, not the grant/refuse decision.
- Priority 1 (higher education): provider has not reached its prioritisation threshold. Priority 2: provider has reached 80% of its New Overseas Student Commencement (NOSC) allocation. Priority 3: provider has exceeded its allocation by 15% (upper threshold).
- Home Affairs "aims to commence processing" P1 within 1-4 weeks, P2 within 5-8 weeks, P3 within 9-12 weeks of lodgement. Based on 2025 lodgements; can change in peak periods.
- The priority is that of the provider of the MAIN CoE, on the day of lodgement. Students are told to note the status on the lodgement day.
- Calendar-year rule: applications lodged 15 November to 31 December are prioritised using the provider's progress toward the FOLLOWING year's allocation. The page's example: lodged 18 Nov 2026 for a degree starting 2027 uses the provider's 2027 status. This is directly relevant to Semester 1 2027 lodgements from 15 Nov 2026.
- Where to check the provider's status for higher education: the Department of Education "Visa Prioritisation Status" page (found by search at education.gov.au; the page itself was not opened). Individual university status for 2026 or 2027 was NOT looked up.
- Study Australia's description: Priority 1 until the provider reaches 80% of allocation; later applications are still accepted but processed more slowly.
- Not found: each of the Group of Eight + UTS/RMIT/Macquarie's 2027 allocation number. An Education Department document "indicative allocations higher education new overseas students commencements 2027" exists (search result URL: https://www.education.gov.au/download/20529/indicative-allocations-higher-education-new-overseas-students-commencements-2027/45254/document/pdf ) but was not opened.
- Taiwan note: the priority categories that jump to Priority 1 regardless of provider (Pacific, TAFE, school students, etc.) do not apply to a Taiwan degree student.

### 3.3 Fee, funds, GS, English, health, biometrics, under-18

Visa application charge
- Reported: AUD 2,500, effective 1 July 2026 (up from AUD 2,000). Seen only in search summaries quoting Study Australia and Home Affairs material; the Home Affairs 500 page loads its price by script and my fetch did not show the number, and a Study Australia news URL I tried returned 404. CONFIRM on the Home Affairs Visa Pricing Estimator before showing a number to students. Search-summary sources: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/tools-and-resources/news/student-visa-application-charge-increase (404 when fetched) and https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/fees-and-charges .
- Lower price applies only to eligible Pacific Island and Timor-Leste citizens, eligible ASEAN citizens and applicants in the ELICOS and Non-Award sector. Taiwan is not in these groups. Source: Home Affairs 500 page, page dated 22/10/2026 (link above).
- The charge that applies depends on the date Home Affairs receives the application (search summary quoting Home Affairs).

Financial capacity (funds)
- Living-cost figure for the primary applicant: AUD 29,710 for 12 months, applicable to applications lodged on or after 10 May 2024. Source: legislative instrument amendment (LIN 24/042, made 9 May 2024), which replaced AUD 24,505: https://legislation.gov.au/F2024L00529/asmade/2024-05-09/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html . Earlier figure AUD 24,505 applied from 1 Oct 2023: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en_in/tools-and-resources/news/change-to-evidence-of-financial-capacity-for-student-visas (dated 6 September 2023).
- The requirement is living costs PLUS first-year tuition PLUS travel (search summary of the instrument). Travel and tuition amounts: not confirmed.
- I found no later change to AUD 29,710, but I did not read the current consolidated instrument. The 30/10/2026 consultation calls it "currently outlined in a legislative instrument" and proposes changes (proposed only).
- Accepted evidence types and how a parent-sponsored Taiwan applicant should show funds: not confirmed here; the Document Checklist Tool decides it.

Genuine Student (GS), what the student writes
- Source (page dated 24/09/2026): https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500/genuine-student-requirement
- The online form asks (paraphrased): (1) current circumstances including ties to family, community, employment and economic circumstances; (2) why this course in Australia with this particular provider, showing understanding of the course and of studying and living in Australia; (3) how completing the course benefits the applicant; (4) anything else relevant. A further question applies to people who have held a student visa or who apply from within Australia.
- Limit of 150 words per response, all in English. Home Affairs prefers answers in the form to a separate GS statement. Statements supported by evidence get more weight.
- What it says to cite: previous study and transcripts; ties to the home country; economic circumstances and income evidence; reasons for not studying a similar course at home; level of research into the course, provider and living arrangements; immigration and visa history; for a minor, the intentions of the parent or guardian.
- "Future intentions" such as later applying for permanent residence "do not count against an applicant under GS".
- For a Taiwanese school leaver the same page's economic-circumstances list is employment-based; whether parents' income documents are the usual evidence is not stated there. Uncertain.

Evidence Level (the successor to assessment levels)
- Home Affairs does not publish a public country list (per search summaries); the Document Checklist Tool decides at lodgement. Taiwan was described as Evidence Level 1 only by a migration-agency blog (September 2026) and was absent from an IDP Level 2 list (September 2025). Unconfirmed officially. Sources: https://www.oneuedu.com/en-US/visa/policy-updates/student-visa-evidence-level-september-2026 (agency, label: secondary) and https://www.idp.com/australia/study-to-migrate/student-visa-assessment-levels-australia/ (agent, secondary).
- Why it matters: the proposed interim measure (3.1) targets only Evidence Level 3 countries.

English for the visa
- Study Australia: only certain English tests are accepted, and whether a student needs test results depends on circumstances and exemptions. The exact visa English rule for a Taiwan degree applicant (for example whether a university's own English acceptance counts): NOT FOUND in this pass.

Health examination
- Home Affairs says health requirements apply; the student uses My Health Declaration to find out whether an examination is required and to arrange it before lodging. Whether a Taiwan passport holder staying more than six months is asked for an examination: NOT FOUND. Source for the general statement: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/visa-application-process.html
- If an English test, biometrics or health examination cannot be completed in time, attach evidence of the steps taken to ImmiAccount before the Home Affairs deadline (Study Australia visa page).

Biometrics: the FAQ above says they can be requested; whether Taiwan applicants are asked, and where to give them: NOT FOUND.

Character: a police certificate may be requested (Study Australia apply page).

Under 18
- An approved welfare arrangement is required for applicants under 18 (Study Australia visa page). Melbourne says an under-18 student must show appropriate accommodation, support and general welfare arrangements BEFORE accepting the offer (Melbourne offers page). ANU has a separate under-18 page and earlier Semester 1 2027 accommodation date (11 January 2027, per search summary): https://study.anu.edu.au/apply/international-students-under-18-years-age
- CAAW (Confirmation of Appropriate Accommodation and Welfare) mechanics, guardian eligibility, and the 590 Student Guardian visa conditions: NOT researched here. Student Guardian 590 rules changed from 2 October 2026 (Home Affairs change page, 14/10/2026); family inclusion is only allowed if an exemption applies.

### 4.2 OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover)
- Required for most 500 holders for the whole stay (unless exempt) and must be arranged before applying. Sources: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/tools-and-resources/tips-and-advice-for-students/oshc-what-international-students-need-to-know (dated 19 May 2026); https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/overseas-student-health-cover-oshc
- Insurers listed by Study Australia: ahm OSHC, Allianz Care Australia (Peoplecare), Bupa, CBHS International Health, Medibank, nib OSHC.
- Basic cover includes GP, hospital treatment, blood tests and X-rays, ambulance, some prescriptions. Dental and optical need Extras. Gap fees can apply. Pharmaceutical benefits are limited (search summary of privatehealth.gov.au: AUD 50 per item, AUD 500 a year for single).
- Cost: NO current figure found. Only a dated one: the lowest-priced single cover for 12 months was AUD 478 as of September 2021 (Lonergan review summarised in a search result; https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-05/overseas-student-health-cover-oshc-review-8-june-2022.pdf ). The tracker should show a live quote link, not a number. Compare at https://privatehealth.gov.au/health_insurance/overseas/overseas_student_health_cover.htm .

## 5. Work rights and post-study visa

Work while studying (IN FORCE; Study Australia visa page, text current after 2 Oct 2026): https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/your-guide-to-visas/student-visa-subclass-500
- Up to 48 hours per fortnight while the course is in session, including exam periods. Unlimited during scheduled course breaks, after the course on the CoE is completed (while the visa is valid), and for research masters and doctoral students after the course starts.
- Cannot work before the course starts unless holding another visa that allows it. Check VEVO or the grant letter for the exact conditions.

Temporary Graduate visa (485), bachelor's graduate
- Post-Higher Education Work stream is for those who recently completed a degree-level or higher qualification in a CRICOS-registered course. General conditions: aged 35 or under at application (exceptions apply), hold an eligible visa, have held a student visa in the last 6 months, apply for the stream matching the qualification (cannot change after applying). Unlimited work hours. Stay length depends on qualification and circumstances. Sources: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-move/your-guide-to-visas/temporary-graduate-visa-subclass-485 (no date) and the Home Affairs 485 page (dated 14/12/2024, mostly script-loaded) https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/temporary-graduate-485
- Duration for a bachelor's graduate: NOT confirmed on a current official page. A search summary of an older Home Affairs help page said 2, 3 or 4 years by qualification. Do not present "2 years" as fact; Hong Kong/BNO holders have a different rule, not applicable to Taiwan.
- 485 fee: a search summary says it rose to AUD 5,750 on 1 July 2026 (from 4,600); another says 485 charges rose from 1 March 2026. Unconfirmed. Confirm on the Home Affairs pricing estimator.
- English requirement and the exact course-completion rules for a student starting in 2027: NOT found.
- Study Australia also lists employer sponsorship and SkillSelect expressions of interest as other routes.

## 6. Pain points and common mistakes

Source type labels: [OFFICIAL] = from a Home Affairs/Study Australia/university page read; [DERIVED] = my inference from official facts; [COMMUNITY] = counselor/student source. No community source was read in this pass, so there are no [COMMUNITY] items.

1. [OFFICIAL] Booking flights before the visa: Study Australia says to allow enough time and "Do not make travel plans based on an assumption that your visa will be granted".
2. [OFFICIAL] CoE must still be valid when Home Affairs decides. If the CoE changes after lodging, the new one must be attached in ImmiAccount.
3. [OFFICIAL] Priority is fixed on the lodgement day by the provider's allocation status, and applications lodged 15 Nov-31 Dec count against the NEXT year's allocation. Lodging in early November versus mid-November can fall under different years' status. [DERIVED] A student who waits until a provider hits 80% may drop to Priority 2 and lose weeks.
4. [OFFICIAL] The fee depends on the date Home Affairs receives the application (search summary quoting Home Affairs); a price rise between lodging and receipt means paying the new price.
5. [OFFICIAL] GS answers: 150-word limit per answer, English only, in the form rather than a separate statement; evidence-backed statements weigh more.
6. [OFFICIAL] Conditional offers: at Melbourne, conditions must be met before accepting; late results need the Conditional CoE route.
7. [OFFICIAL] Under 18: welfare arrangements must exist before accepting at Melbourne.
8. [OFFICIAL] Work: no work before the course starts; 48 hours per fortnight in session.
9. [OFFICIAL] Scholarships: Sydney VCIS requires an unconditional offer by the round date; UNSW's Term 1 2027 window closed 30 Oct 2026; Melbourne's main undergraduate award excludes Taiwan by GDP rule.
10. [DERIVED] Rules in flux: GS (Ministerial Direction 106) and financial capacity are under consultation to 23 Nov 2026; students lodging in 2027 may face changed evidence.
